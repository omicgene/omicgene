# Contributor Covenant Code of Conduct & Scientific Research Ethics

## Our Pledge

We as members, contributors, and leaders pledge to make participation in the **OmicGene** research community a harassment-free, intellectually honest, and inclusive experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, caste, color, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming, diverse, inclusive, healthy, and scientifically rigorous community.

---

## Scientific Integrity & Open Science Principles

As a research-driven organization operating in the domain of computational genomics and antimicrobial resistance:

1. **Academic Honesty**: Plagiarism, falsification of data, fabrication of benchmark results, and selective omission of negative experimental findings are strictly prohibited.
2. **Reproducibility**: All algorithmic claims and model checkpoints submitted to OmicGene must be accompanied by sufficient experimental specifications, random seeds, and documentation to allow independent replication.
3. **Data Privacy & Bioethics**: Contributors must strictly ensure that no confidential clinical records, unconsented patient health information (PHI), or protected genomic identifiers are uploaded. All genomic datasets must conform to internationally recognized open access standards (e.g. NCBI SRA, ENA, DDBJ).
4. **Constructive Peer Review**: Code reviews, algorithm assessments, and issue discussions should be conducted with mutual respect, objective argumentation, and intellectual rigor.

## Our Standards

Examples of behavior that contributes to a positive environment for our community include:

* Demonstrating empathy and kindness toward other people
* Being respectful of differing opinions, viewpoints, and scientific hypotheses
* Giving and gracefully accepting constructive feedback
* Accepting responsibility and apologizing to those affected by our mistakes, and learning from the experience
* Focusing on what is best not just for us as individuals, but for the overall research community

Examples of unacceptable behavior include:

* The use of sexualized language or imagery, and sexual attention or advances of any kind
* Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks
* Public or private harassment
* Publishing others' private information, such as a physical or email address, without their explicit permission
* Other conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting

---

## Enforcement Responsibilities

Community leaders are responsible for clarifying and enforcing our standards of acceptable behavior and will take appropriate and fair corrective action in response to any behavior that they deem inappropriate, threatening, offensive, or harmful.

Community leaders have the right and responsibility to remove, edit, or reject comments, commits, code, wiki edits, issues, and other contributions that are not aligned to this Code of Conduct, and will communicate reasons for moderation decisions when appropriate.

---

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies within all community spaces, and also applies when an individual is officially representing the community in public spaces. Examples of representing our community include using an official e-mail address, posting via an official social media account, or acting as an appointed representative at an online or offline event.
